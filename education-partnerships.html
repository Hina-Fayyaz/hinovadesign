Five connected challenges

The problem is bigger than making content look better.

Pakistan needs more children in education—and stronger learning once they arrive. Hinova's contribution is focused: make approved learning clearer, more active, more usable and easier to improve.

01

Access is deeply unequal

The government estimate puts out-of-school rates at 21% in Punjab and 45% in Balochistan. Nationally, the rate is higher for girls (31%) than boys (25%).

Learning-design response Materials must work across languages, locations, devices and print—not assume one ideal classroom.
02

Attendance does not guarantee learning

Learning poverty is estimated at 77%. Enrolment matters, but students also need guided practice, feedback and opportunities to apply knowledge.

Learning-design response Every module should connect an outcome to practice, feedback and evidence of learning.
03

Infrastructure changes the design brief

Only 65% of public schools have electricity overall; the figure is 21% in Balochistan. Digital-only solutions can widen the gap they intend to close.

Learning-design response Use print-ready, mobile-friendly and low-bandwidth formats with clear offline pathways.
04

Degrees need stronger links to practice

HEC’s mandatory internship and professional-certification requirements signal a national push toward employability and applied experience.

Learning-design response Courses need authentic briefs, portfolio evidence, rubrics and structured reflection—not content alone.
05

Change depends on faculty adoption

New tools and AI do not improve learning by themselves. Educators need usable guides, examples, review time and sustained support.

Learning-design response Design the faculty experience alongside the student experience, then improve it through a pilot.

A low-risk starting point

Start with one course. Build proof before you scale.

90 days One course · one cohort · one measurable priority
  1. 01
    Weeks 1–2

    Diagnose

    Select one course, one learner group and one priority. Review outcomes, content, assessment, constraints and faculty workflow.

  2. 02
    Weeks 3–4

    Blueprint

    Map approved outcomes to modules, practice, assessment and delivery formats. Agree what evidence the pilot will collect.

  3. 03
    Weeks 5–8

    Prototype

    Build a representative learning unit and faculty toolkit. Academic reviewers verify accuracy before student use.

  4. 04
    Weeks 9–12

    Pilot + improve

    Test with a real cohort, capture learner and faculty evidence, refine the system and recommend the next scale decision.

Decide with evidence

What a pilot can measure

  • Change between a short baseline and exit task
  • Completion and participation in designed activities
  • Quality of applied work against a shared rubric
  • Learner clarity, usability and confidence
  • Faculty preparation time and implementation feedback

Practical questions

Before we begin

A useful pilot has clear roles, realistic constraints and an honest way to judge progress.

Does Hinova replace curriculum experts or faculty? +

No. Subject experts and the institution retain academic authority. Hinova structures the learner journey, designs the materials and supports implementation; academic reviewers approve accuracy and alignment.

Can one design solution work across Pakistan? +

Not responsibly without adaptation. A pilot identifies learner language, access, device, print and faculty constraints. We then create a core system with format or context variants where they are genuinely needed.

Can you design bilingual materials? +

Yes, when terminology and translation are provided or approved by qualified institutional reviewers. Hinova can design a consistent bilingual system and make both versions easy to use.

Can you guarantee better learning outcomes? +

No ethical partner should guarantee an outcome before testing. Hinova designs for clear outcomes, active practice and better usability, then helps the institution measure the pilot’s contribution and decide what to improve.

What do you need to start? +

A course or program owner, approved outcomes or standards, sample content, a named academic reviewer, learner context, delivery constraints and access to a small pilot group.
